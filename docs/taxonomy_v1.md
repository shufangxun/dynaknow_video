# DynaKnow-Video v1 Taxonomy

Use this taxonomy to collect videos and construct answer choices. Each final item should have one primary category and one primary knowledge point.

## Category A: Physics and Mechanics

Candidate knowledge points:

- Unsupported objects accelerate downward under gravity.
- Objects sliding on rough surfaces slow down due to friction.
- Collisions transfer momentum between objects.
- Non-elastic collisions dissipate kinetic energy.
- A pendulum exchanges gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy.
- Buoyancy depends on displaced fluid and object density.
- Air resistance slows moving objects.
- An object on an inclined plane accelerates along the slope.
- Elastic materials restore shape after deformation.
- A spinning object can maintain angular momentum.

Good video patterns:

- Falling object.
- Rolling ball on ramp.
- Cart collision.
- Pendulum swing.
- Floating/sinking object.
- Spring or rubber band release.

## Category B: Chemistry and Material Change

Candidate knowledge points:

- A solid can absorb heat and melt into a liquid.
- A liquid can evaporate when heated or exposed to airflow.
- Dissolved substances diffuse from high to low concentration.
- Acid-carbonate reactions can release carbon dioxide gas bubbles.
- Persulfate iodine-clock reactions turn dark blue after thiosulfate is depleted and iodine binds starch.
- Benedict's reagent changes color when heated with reducing sugars.
- Mixing ions that form an insoluble salt can produce a precipitate.
- Materials can expand unevenly when heated.
- Combustion requires fuel and oxygen.
- Cooling can cause liquid to solidify.
- Solvent evaporation can leave crystals behind.

Good video patterns:

- Ice melting.
- Colored liquid diffusion.
- Carbon dioxide bubbles from an acid-carbonate reaction.
- Iodine-clock or Benedict color-test endpoint change.
- Insoluble salt precipitate forming after two solutions mix.
- Bimetal strip bending.
- Crystallization over time.

Do not use generic chemistry targets such as "some reactions change color," "some reactions form a precipitate," or "some mixtures produce gas." If the reaction class or mechanism cannot be named, reject the candidate or keep it for later refinement rather than generating a final QA item.

## Category C: Biology and Life Processes

Candidate knowledge points:

- Plant shoots can grow toward a light source.
- Roots can grow downward in response to gravity.
- Water can move through narrow plant structures by capillary action.
- Transpiration helps move water through plants.
- Muscles move joints by contraction and pulling.
- A pump-like organ can drive fluid circulation.
- Some organisms move toward or away from light.
- Seed germination involves root and shoot emergence.
- Leaves can lose water vapor through stomata.
- Animal movement often uses repeated joint cycles.

Good video patterns:

- Time-lapse plant bending.
- Seed germination.
- Colored water moving through celery/stem.
- Heart pump model.
- Joint/muscle model.
- Insect moving toward light.

## Category D: Everyday Causal Mechanisms

Candidate knowledge points:

- Porous materials absorb liquid through internal spaces.
- Warm air rises and can drive convection.
- Evaporation can cool a surface.
- Magnetic fields can align or attract ferromagnetic materials.
- Pressure differences can move fluids.
- Capillary action can pull liquid through narrow gaps.
- Reduced friction can cause slipping.
- Center of mass affects whether an object tips over.
- Surface tension can support or reshape small liquid structures.
- Vibration can transfer energy through a medium.

Good video patterns:

- Sponge absorbing water.
- Candle/heat moving paper or smoke upward.
- Magnet and iron filings.
- Straw/syringe pressure demo.
- Water climbing paper towel.
- Slipping tire or object.

## Category E: Procedural and Operational Principles

Candidate knowledge points:

- Fixing an object before cutting or drilling reduces motion error.
- Gradual heating can reduce thermal shock.
- Stirring while adding powder improves dispersion.
- Venting before sealing can reduce pressure buildup.
- Sequential tightening can distribute force evenly.
- Pre-wetting can reduce absorption during later application.
- Filtering separates solids from liquids by particle size.
- Settling separates materials by density over time.
- Layering fluids can demonstrate density differences.
- Repeated small adjustments can stabilize a system better than one large adjustment.

Good video patterns:

- Cutting after clamping.
- Glass heated gradually vs suddenly.
- Powder mixed while stirring.
- Venting container before sealing.
- Filtration.
- Density column.
